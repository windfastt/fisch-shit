Prove the quadratic formula.

That is, let $a, b, c \in \mathbb{C}$ (or $\mathbb{R}$) with $a \neq 0$. Prove that for any $x$,

$$ax^2 + bx + c = 0 \iff x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$

where $\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}$ denotes a square root of the discriminant $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$ (and over $\mathbb{R}$, real solutions exist if and only if $\Delta \geq 0$).
